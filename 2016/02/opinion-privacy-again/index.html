opinion: privacy, again

16 Feb, 2016 • 1638 words • 8 min • updated 14 Nov, 2026

⚠️ This post is over one year old. It may no longer be up to date or relevant. Opinions may have changed.

(Digital) life is always a battle between security (privacy) and convenience (being practical). The line between these two opposing styles is becoming subtler and subtler each day and it is more and more difficult to determine which is the best behavior to adopt1.

In this (short)2 post I will compare my current opinion about this theme with the one my alter ego had +/- a year ago. Please pay special attention to the contrast (or fixation) between my opinions.

Quick Introduction (overall) #

Past: heavy user of {Arch,Gentoo} Linux, would use Firefox, would use adblock + a half dozen of privacy-enhancing add-ons + keep the system up-to-date every day, would sign up for mailing lists of his operating system.

Current: user of OS X3, light user of bloated Linux distributions4, would use ublock (great!) in Google Chrome5

  • one or two privacy-enhancing complements (okay) + keep the system up-to-date on a weekly basis (okay, supposing you don’t expose your computer to the wild carelessly), does not care about the Apple community(overall).

Software no one cares about #

Past: would not install Java, only if extremely needed; would not install Flash, only if wanted to play games in Kongregate…7.

Current: Java: mostly the same; Flash: the same however I have Flash in Chrome (not in the whole system) since it is built-in.

Add-ons you should care about #

I won’t provide links as they might become deprecated. Search for them:

  • get a good adblocker (nowadays: either ublock or adblock plus)
  • get a good https enforcer (‘https everywhere’)
  • get a good search engine (I like to use DuckDuckGo + Google8. Another alternative is StartPage). Probably Google is the only company I don’t care/mind as much being tracked by (as others).
  • (optional): get a “anti-tracker”. Common options nowadays are ghostery and EFF’s privacy badger. There is also lightbeam for Firefox but AFAIK it doesn’t block – it merely collects stats.

Social Networks #

Aha. This is the most interesting (and the last) part of this post:

Past: too much careful about Facebook9, hates Instagram (a network for posers and humblebragging), has never heard of Snapchat (or has heard bad things about it), casual Twitter user10, light user of Telegram messaging.

Current: loosely careful about Facebook11, casual Instagram user12, a common snapchat user13, opens Twitter one time per week or so10, heavy user of Telegram (great!).

And, finally, Telegram is an amazing app for messaging. It is open source, has groups, and it supports encryption should you need it.

Conclusion #

Do whatever you want, I don’t care14. But please, please, please: be aware and conscious of your surroundings. The web is becoming more and more ubiquitous and public. When in doubt, prefer to be private. However if you are being way too much private to the extent of not enjoying all of the capabilities and/or opportunities you could have with a given platform or social network, then consider being slightly more open. And live on. Life is all about finding the right balance. ∎

  9. Facebook privacy should be important, however you don’t want to become insane by pretending it works like snapchat and (thus) deleting your old posts or events you attended. The better thing to do is: think twice before posting so you won’t have to clea{r,n} your posts later. The way better thing is: don’t share flood or cheap humor (aka 9gag, buzzfeed, and similar), at least not too often, as it pollutes your timeline and the timeline of your friends. There are better places and/or communities more suitable to share these kind of posts (I just cited two of them). Also, don’t post all of your photos to Facebook. It you really want to post some photos, then just post a few ones. There are people that upload entire albums to Facebook as it were super cool. Not only it is lazy but it also permanently exposes every moment of your life/trip/whatever to every person with access to your feed, and there is no easy “undo” button – you can delete the photos later, but they will still be stored somewhere in Facebook servers. ↩︎

  10. in 2009 I would be super enthusiastic about twitter (or even ‘identica’, for that matter). Today I simply lost almost all of my attraction for it. It is still cool, but it is not for me, neither as a follower or as a tweeter. However, for those of you who still like it (which is okay), I would recommend deleting old tweets periodically (there are several services which do that) or protecting your tweets, as they quickly become deprecated in relation to what you tend to think. ↩︎ ↩︎

  11. I think the best way to use Facebook is to be flexible and maximize your (induced) serendipity of getting good events to go to and good connections to interact with (a.k.a. ‘friends’ in the Facebook terminology) without being too much restrictive, but without being too much open either. Finding the right balance is tricky and it is a matter of experience and personal choice. Anyway, regardless of how you use the network, it is really bad to be too much closed (why bother having an account then? I know some people who have not. They are perfectly normal and sane. Yes, they are real too!) and also to be too much open (you don’t want all of your posts or photos indexed by search engines or way too much addressable. Right? Specially, you don’t want your new friends (maybe your future boss, who knows?) to see things you don’t want them to see). ↩︎

  12. Instagram is tricky. Yeah, I find out its design awesome (congrats engineers!) from the technical point of view. But I find out its users either (i)way too much open (and sometimes unaware of that) or (ii)way too much ‘I want to be pop, and brag all the time’ or (iii)‘I want to document all of my trips every day or so, even if these trips are from weeks or months ago’ or (iv)whatever. The point is: it is really hard to pick a subset of its users who try to use the site/app as a place to share good moments and feelings for a true and pure purpose (what is true or pure anyway?) other than just trying to appear. Well, people do what they wanna do and whatever, but still if you have to define a metric to determine what is a good community, the items I discussed here will inevitably be discussed. I am still unsure whether I identify myself with the overall Instagram community, however I learned that if the app is well designed (it is indeed!) and if it was made for the purpose of sharing occasional photos (not posting entire albums, please!), then it is a good fit for a person who wants do to exactly that: “share occasional photos”. Regardless of how other people use it. And period. Oh, and about privacy: keep photos private. I think I won’t change my mind about that point. ↩︎

  13. almost finally: snapchat. It is a very original and nice app IMHO. First, photos are temporary, which is greeeeeeaat (no digital traces, at least theoretically); second, you share small moments of your life with people you (theoretically) care about, and you get a snapshot of the life of people who (again – theoretically) you care about. It is private by nature and there is no way of accessing its photos from the web (I mean, unlike Instagram; of course you could try to scrape the origin of the photos by debugging/tracing the web requests from the app but let’s focus on the overall end-user). The “small” moment concept is great and probably inherited or influenced by Twitter at some point (and maybe this is the reason why people don’t seem to use Twitter anymore as much as in the past). And well, I think if it is used wisely and/or with attention, then it is the best social network out there today. Less superficial than twitter / facebook and less bragging-wise than Instagram (well, at least people can’t post old photos/videos in there). And, the most important: it feels personal and interactive – exactly what a social network aims to be (usually). ↩︎

  14. ‘unfollow’ is always a great concept for stop caring should you need to ↩︎
