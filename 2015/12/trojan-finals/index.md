I have to say I am very surprised (and happy). I thought this *finals* thing was
just a casual[^1] way to refer to the latest exams of the semester, but I
realized it is way more than that; there is a big tradition, and I'd dare to say
*community* too, around them here at USC.

First, everything is [unified](http://classes.usc.edu/term-20153/finals/), so
that all of the final exams are held in the same one-week period. This also
ensures that no two exams are going to take place at the time time[^2].

Second, there are several events related to "relaxing", and/or "taking a break"
and/or "boosting your study performance": the faculty really want their students
to do well. There is no such thing as doing a final exam just for the sake of
doing the final exam; instead, it just feels something natural do be done
anyway, after almost 4 months of continuous learning in a very positive and
friendly environment and context. A remarkable example is the fact that the USC
dining halls have expended three consecutive dawns (12am - 2am) open slightly
before the final exam week, doing a kind of "late breakfast"[^3], so students
could keep studying – full of energy, yaaaay – late at night.

Finally, there is *at least* a whole week between the end of classes and the
beginning of the final exam week, devoted just for studies – well, in the end
you **cannot** really create an
[excuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_syndrome) that you didn't have
time for studying[^4].

Now it comes the last statement: this is so **freaking highly superior** as a
method of maturity and cognitive psychology improvement and well-being compared
to the de-facto standard thing I see in Brazil. Here the opportunity for one to
grow {fast} as a learner is *so big* that, if I did a direct comparison, it
simply would be [unfair](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pc9mQILZno4).
